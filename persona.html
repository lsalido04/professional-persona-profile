Part 01

Core Values Exploration

My Radian results helped me connect what motivates me with the kind of career I want. Financial Interest was my strongest interest area, which fits my goal of gaining experience at a bank or investment firm. I want to understand how financial decisions are made and eventually bring that experience back to my family’s business.

Achievement, helping others, and understanding how things work also stood out in my ratings. For me, those priorities connect to responsibility, curiosity, and growth. At Porcinas Dosali, supporting inventory and financial records gives me a practical reason to care about accurate information: other people depend on it to make decisions.

I want to keep building technical skills while remembering who the work supports. A useful solution should improve a process and make someone’s job easier.

Part 02

Behavioral Communication Research Findings (SPI/PRS)

My Self-Perception Inventory identified my natural communication style as Expressive, with proactivity of 2.0 and reactivity of 3.0. I relate to the emphasis on new ideas, sharing perspectives, and being open to coaching. My preferred team role, Innovation, also fits my interest in improving the way work gets done.

The Professional Reputation Survey dashboard also labels my average behavioral style Expressive. Across three responses, it reports proactivity of 6.3 and reactivity of 0.3. Compared with my self-assessment, those scores suggest that others experience me as more assertive and less focused on emoting and interacting than I see myself.

My next step is to make my enthusiasm useful to the whole team: ask for others’ ideas before pushing my own, keep explanations focused, and end discussions with clear responsibilities. In finance, I want to combine that initiative with careful listening and evidence.

Part 03

Team Culture Findings (SPI/360°)

My preferred informal team role is Innovation. I enjoy looking for patterns, noticing when an approach needs to change, and coming up with ways to solve a problem. Our team’s SPI summary described an Analytical communication style, with average proactivity of -0.5 and reactivity of -0.33. That reminds me to explain the reasoning behind an idea and give teammates time to consider it.

The team role results included two Results members, two Process members, one Relationships member, and me in Innovation. No one selected Pragmatist. To help cover that gap, I plan to check ideas against our time, resources, and assignment requirements before we commit.

My own work preferences favor direct communication and approachable leadership. I also value getting to know teammates beyond tasks. Together, those preferences remind me to be clear about the work while making room for the relationships that help a team function.

Part 04

Growth Mindset

One area I want to improve is making my contribution more consistent and visible during team projects. Radian listed my individual presentation evaluation as Low Meets Expectations for Stakeholder Analysis & Benchmark and High Meets Expectations for Client Reputation Analysis. That change is encouraging, but I still want to keep improving how I prepare and communicate.

The Expressive style description also gave me something to watch: enthusiasm can take up too much space in a conversation or lead to ideas that are more complicated than the task needs. I want to keep bringing ideas while being more deliberate about listening and following through.

For my next project, I plan to agree on my responsibilities early, share a draft before the team deadline, and ask a teammate for one specific improvement. After the presentation, I will review the feedback and choose one change to carry into the next project.

Part 05

Identity–Brand–Reputation

I see myself as curious, confident, competitive, and interested in solving problems. Those words are part of my identity, but my reputation depends on how people experience working with me. In my three-response Professional Reputation Survey, Hard-Working appeared three times; Competitive, Curious, Self-Confident, and Social each appeared twice.

That overlap is a useful starting point. I want my professional brand to show that I can connect business needs with technology and take responsibility for the details. My MIS coursework and operations experience give me a foundation to build on, while my portfolio gives recruiters examples they can evaluate.

My main audience is hiring managers and early-career recruiters at banks and investment firms, especially in business analysis and operations. I want them to see potential backed by clear examples and contact me about an internship. Over time, I hope to bring what I learn to my family’s company.
